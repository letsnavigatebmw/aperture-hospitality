How to use this calculator

Ten minutes from blank to a pricing decision you can defend

Build a comparison

  1. Set up the SKU. Name the product, then enter bottles per case and your landed cost of goods per bottle: liquid, glass, closure, labels, bottling and freight to the U.S.
  2. Enter the route to market. Distributor margin is a share of the price to retail; retailer margin is a share of the shelf price. Add freight and state taxes between your FOB and the distributor's landed cost, and the federal excise you pay.
  3. Enter today's shelf price and volume. The first card is your current position: the shelf price consumers see and the cases you expect to sell in a year.
  4. Model two alternatives. Scenario A and Scenario B are your what-ifs, for example a promotional price that should lift volume, or a premium repositioning that trades cases for margin. Click a card's title to rename it.
  5. Choose how partners react. Most distributors and retailers hold their margin percentage, so you fund about half of any price cut. If they hold their dollar margin instead, you fund all of it. Switch between the two above the cards.
  6. Stress-test and share. Use the sensitivity grid to see how gross profit moves if COGS or the shelf price lands off plan, then click Copy summary for a plain-text recap.

Before you decide

  • Margins vary by state and account. Use the margins your distributor actually applies in the market you're pricing, and check control-state rules separately.
  • Gross profit isn't the whole cost of a deal. Depletion allowances, sampling, slotting and marketing tied to a price move all raise the volume you need.
  • Your inputs stay in this browser. Nothing is sent to Aperture, and anyone else who opens the link sees the example figures. Use Copy summary to share your numbers.
